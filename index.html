The weekly minimum

To hit 10 lb in four weeks you need roughly 300–400 kcal of deliberate movement most days. That's a 45–60 minute brisk walk. Nothing exotic required.

  • 5–6 days: 45–60 min walking, incline if you have it
  • 3 days: 25–30 min of resistance work — push-ups, squats, rows, carries. This is what keeps the lost weight from being muscle.
  • Daily: a 10-minute walk after your two meals. Small, but it blunts the post-meal glucose spike and adds up to ~700 kcal a week.

Wearables and machines overestimate burn by 15–30%. The numbers here are deliberately conservative — don't eat back more than half of them.

What you're actually burning

Reading the scale

The first week will look spectacular and part of it is a lie. Dropping carbs and calories flushes glycogen, and each gram of stored glycogen holds ~3 g of water. Expect 3–5 lb of that in week one. It's real weight but it isn't fat, and it doesn't repeat.

After that, the honest rate is 1.5–2.5 lb a week. A flat or up day means nothing. Judge only the 7-day average, and only against last week's 7-day average.

Salt, a late dinner, a hard workout, and poor sleep can each swing you 2–3 lb overnight. None of it is fat.

The plan

Your numbers, your coffee, your fasting schedule

Can you do 16 lb in 4 weeks?

10 lb: yes, very likely. 16 lb in 28 days: no — not as real weight you keep. Here's the arithmetic, so you can see it rather than take my word.

16 lb of fat is about 56,000 calories. Spread over 28 days that's a 2,000 kcal deficit every single day — roughly your entire daily burn. You'd have to eat nothing. What actually happens at that intensity is muscle loss, a stalled metabolism, and a rebound.

What is achievable: about 2–2.5 lb/week of fat plus a 3–5 lb water drop in week one. That lands you around 195–199 lb on day 28 — your 10 lb goal, comfortably beaten.

The honest timeline to 190
190.3 lb is a genuinely good target — it puts your BMI at 24.7, inside the healthy range for 6'1.5". At a sustainable rate you get there in 6–7 weeks, not 4. Same destination. Keep the app running past day 28.

The coffee question

You drink 1.5 cups of decaf with about 0.5 cup (120 ml) of oat milk. At 110 kcal and 6 g fat per 240 ml, your actual pour costs:

Your coffee, as you make it55 kcal · 3 g fat
Two of them in a day110 kcal · 6 g fat
Three165 kcal · 9 g fat

The verdict: two a day, capped. 110 kcal is 7% of your daily allowance and 6 g is 12% of your fat budget — affordable, and it's the one ritual you shouldn't have to negotiate. A third is fine on a training day. Four starts eating a meal.

Timing is the real rule
Oat milk breaks your fast. 55 kcal with carbohydrate in it is enough to blunt the fasted state. So: black decaf only before noon — unlimited, it costs ~3 kcal and genuinely helps with hunger. Save the oat milk coffee for 12:00, alongside your first meal, and the second one at 3–4 pm where it doubles as a snack and gets you to dinner.

If you want a third pour, swap the oat milk for a splash of water and drink it black, or halve the oat milk to 60 ml (28 kcal, 1.5 g fat).

One caution: decaf still has 2–15 mg caffeine per cup, so three cups late is not a sleep problem — but oat milk after 6 pm is 55 calories you'll wish you'd spent on dinner.

Fasting: pick one, run it four weeks

Fasting doesn't burn fat by magic. It works because it shrinks the hours in which you can eat, which for most people cuts 300–500 calories a day without any counting. That's the whole mechanism. Since you already skip breakfast, you're most of the way into protocol A already.

Two meals or three?

Two whole meals plus one deliberate protein snack. That's the answer.

There is nothing metabolically special about three meals — meal frequency has been tested repeatedly and, with calories held equal, it doesn't change fat loss. So don't force breakfast in just because it's called breakfast. Forcing a meal you don't want is how people quit in week two.

But there is one real problem with eating only twice, and it isn't calories — it's protein. Your target is about 150 g a day. Split across two meals that's 75 g each, which is 9 oz of chicken at both sittings. Most people can't sustain that, so they quietly undershoot, and undershooting protein while in a steep deficit is exactly how you lose muscle instead of fat.

So the structure is
12:00 — real meal, 45–50 g protein  ·  15:30 — protein snack, 20–25 g (ham roll-ups, tuna on romaine, two eggs)  ·  19:00 — real meal, 45–55 g protein. Three eating occasions, but only two of them are cooking.

The other advantage of the mid-afternoon snack is practical: it's the difference between arriving at dinner hungry and arriving at dinner ravenous, and the second one is what wrecks the day.

If you truly want only two: make the 3:30 slot a shake or 6 oz of canned tuna — 30 seconds, not a meal — and push both real meals to 55 g protein each. Also drop your fat target to 45 g, because with two meals you'll want the calories in food volume rather than oil.

Working with your groceries

Your pantry is already close to ideal for this — lean protein, chiles, beans, a lot of vegetables, and two sauces (salsa verde and guajillo) that add enormous flavour for almost no calories. Very little needs to change. What does:

  • EVOO is the biggest lever in your kitchen. 120 kcal and 13.5 g fat per tablespoon — a generous free pour can be 300 kcal you never notice. Measure it with a teaspoon, every time. One teaspoon per person per dish.
  • Cheddar and avocado are your other two fat sources. One ounce of cheddar is 9 g fat; half an avocado is 11 g. Pick one of those on any given day, not both, or your 50 g fat target is gone before dinner.
  • The ciabatta rolls are 240–270 kcal each. Not banned — but treat one roll as half a meal, or use half a roll. They're the single densest item on your list.
  • Your pinto beans are a genuine asset. Cooked in water with no fat, they're ~245 kcal and 15 g fiber per cup. Fiber plus protein is the most filling combination there is. Use them where you'd otherwise use rice.
  • Dashi is nearly free. 25 kcal, zero fat, and it turns any pile of vegetables and chicken into a real meal. Lean on it hard.
  • White rice → brown, or halve the portion. A cup of white rice is 205 kcal. Half a cup with double the vegetables looks like the same bowl.

Worth adding to the cart: plain Greek yogurt (protein per calorie is unbeatable), frozen vegetables (volume with no prep), cucumber, and cooking spray to replace half your EVOO pours.

Non-negotiables

  • Protein at every eating occasion. Under-eating protein in a deficit costs you muscle, and muscle is what keeps this off.
  • Don't go under 1,500 kcal. The plan is 1,650. Cutting further doesn't speed it up meaningfully and it does make you quit.
  • 3–4 litres of water a day. Fasting plus a deficit dehydrates you, and thirst reliably masquerades as hunger around 4 pm.
  • Sleep 7+ hours. Short sleep raises ghrelin and measurably increases how much people eat the next day. It's the cheapest intervention on this list.
  • Salt your food. Cutting carbs flushes sodium with the water. Under-salting during a fast is what causes the headaches and the flat feeling people blame on fasting itself.
  • Weigh daily, judge weekly.

This is a general plan built from your numbers, not medical advice. If you take medication — particularly for blood pressure or diabetes — or have any heart, kidney, or blood sugar condition, run the fasting schedule past your doctor first, because meal timing can interact with dosing.

How syncing works here

Your data lives in the browser on the device you're using — GitHub Pages can host the app but can't store anything for you. So phone and laptop each keep their own copy, and you move between them with a file.

  1. Create a new repository on GitHub. Public is fine — there's nothing sensitive in the file itself.
  2. Upload this file and make sure it's named index.html at the top level.
  3. Repository → Settings → Pages → set Source to Deploy from a branch, branch main, folder / (root). Save.
